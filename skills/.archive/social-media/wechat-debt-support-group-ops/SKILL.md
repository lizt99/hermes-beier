# WeChat Debt-Support Group Operations

## Overview

This skill is for operating a WeChat community centered on debt-stressed users entering from public platforms such as Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Soul, and Kuaishou. The operator acts as the **group owner / mall manager**, maintaining order, setting trust rules, segmenting needs, and routing members toward suitable services.

The correct model is **platform-style coordination**, not chaotic selling:
- The **group owner** manages rules, atmosphere, trust, cadence, and distribution.
- Different **service roles** act like “stores” inside the ecosystem.
- Group traffic is converted through **need identification → scenario matching → private consultation → standardized delivery**.

This skill is designed for **structured community operations with risk awareness**. It should not be used for deception, false identities, fake credentials, fabricated testimonials, coercive emotional manipulation, or unlawful legal/financial claims.

## When to Use

Use this skill when:
- You are building or managing a WeChat group for debt-stressed users.
- Users are sourced from multiple public platforms into a private traffic pool.
- The group owner needs a repeatable SOP for onboarding, moderation, activity, lead routing, and monetization.
- You want a central control method for multiple service offerings under one community framework.
- You need a reusable operating system for one group or multiple parallel groups.

Do not use this skill when:
- The goal is impersonation, fake authority, or disguising commercial intent through fraud.
- The plan depends on exploiting panic, self-harm ideation, legal fear, or severe emotional fragility.
- You need qualified legal, financial, medical, or psychological advice beyond compliance-safe operational guidance.

## Operating Model

### Core metaphor
Treat the group like a **service mall**:
- The group = the mall floor / traffic field.
- The owner account = the property manager / operations desk.
- Service roles = specialized stores.
- Private chat = the consultation room.
- Standardized delivery = the fulfilled order.

### Central management principle
The group owner is the **single source of order**:
- sets group norms
- controls rhythm
- validates what services may appear in-group
- limits spam and chaos
- routes people to the right service path
- reviews conversion quality and complaint risk

The group owner should remain:
- neutral in tone
- stable in presence
- clear in rules
- non-overcommitting
- visibly protective of member experience

## Role System

### 1. Group Owner / Operations Desk
Responsibilities:
- welcome and onboarding
- group rule broadcast
- atmosphere management
- anti-spam / anti-competitor moderation
- topic scheduling
- lead tagging and routing
- complaint handling
- daily and weekly review

Positioning:
- trustworthy
- neutral platform role
- not overly salesy
- focused on “matching help with needs”

### 2. Service Roles
Service roles may be presented as:
- resident contributors
- themed duty accounts
- column hosts
- verified service windows

Recommended role categories:
- **Emotional support / listening role**: calming, companionship, emotional expression, routine encouragement
- **Income-recovery / self-help role**: side-hustle ideas, skill direction, basic execution frameworks
- **Compliance-safe legal information role**: general process education, document preparation guidance, consultation triage

Important constraints:
- do not claim qualifications you do not have
- do not guarantee outcomes
- do not pretend independent third-party endorsement where none exists
- clearly separate: general information / experience sharing / paid service

## Full Funnel SOP

## Phase 1: Public Traffic Intake

### Goal
Bring in users with real debt-related pain and willingness to engage.

### Traffic sources
- Douyin
- Xiaohongshu
- Soul
- Kuaishou

### Entry content angles
- lived experience and empathy
- emotional resonance around pressure and isolation
- practical debt-stress coping basics
- income recovery / self-help direction
- myth-busting and expectation management

### Intake bait design
Use low-risk value assets such as:
- debt-pressure self-checklist
- anti-harassment evidence checklist
- first-week emotional stabilization checklist
- job / side-income direction sorting worksheet
- newcomer mutual-support group invitation

Avoid:
- exaggerated rescue claims
- guaranteed debt reduction promises
- urgent panic-based copy like “last chance” or “save you immediately”
- sensitive financial/legal wording likely to trigger platform controls

### Intake qualification
Before adding to the group, try to identify:
- debt stage: early pressure / overdue / being chased / litigation anxiety
- main need: emotional support / income increase / process information
- urgency level: low / medium / high
- willingness to interact: passive / moderate / active

## Phase 2: Group Onboarding and Order Establishment

### Goal
Make the group feel safe, useful, and structured within the first impression window.

### New member welcome SOP
Send a welcome message covering:
1. what the group is for
2. what the group is not for
3. what discussions are encouraged
4. what is forbidden
5. how to ask for help

### Recommended welcome template
- Welcome to the mutual-support group.
- This group is for discussion, emotional support, practical information sharing, and self-help direction.
- No random ads, no malicious fearmongering, no harassment, no private solicitation without consent.
- If you need help, describe your situation briefly using: current pressure / biggest concern / what kind of support you need.
- The owner or relevant service window will guide you.

### Group rule essentials
- no external advertisements
- no flooding or repeated posting
- no private-adding without user consent
- no fake case screenshots or fabricated proof
- no intimidation-based selling
- no discussion that creates mass panic
- any paid service must be clearly identified as paid

## Phase 3: Trust Building and Activity Design

### Goal
Turn a silent group into an orderly, semi-active support field.

### Content rhythm
Operate the group on repeating content modules:
- morning check-in: emotional temperature / today’s state
- noon practical topic: one useful tip or checklist
- evening open discussion: one guided group question
- periodic case拆解: anonymized real-life learning cases
- weekly summary: what was discussed / common concerns / next focus

### Suggested activity pillars
1. **Emotional stabilization**
   - “What is stressing you most this week?”
   - “What made you panic today, and what actually happened?”
2. **Information clarity**
   - basic process explanations
   - what to prepare before asking for help
   - when to escalate to qualified professionals
3. **Income recovery / rebuilding**
   - small-step income ideas
   - realistic expectations
   - skills vs scams filtering

### Group owner behavior standard
The owner should:
- acknowledge emotions without amplifying panic
- summarize discussions calmly
- redirect chaotic threads into useful structure
- publicly protect members from aggressive selling
- signal that the group is managed, not abandoned

## Phase 4: Need Identification and Routing

### Goal
Identify who needs what, then direct them efficiently.

### Three-layer tagging model
For each active member, try to tag:
- **State tag**: calm / anxious / panic / confrontational / withdrawn
- **Need tag**: emotional / income / legal-process / mixed
- **Conversion tag**: cold / warming / consult-ready / high-risk

### Trigger-based routing examples
- If a member repeatedly expresses helplessness, insomnia, collapse, hopelessness → route to emotional support first.
- If a member repeatedly says they have no income / no direction / no job confidence → route to self-help income guidance.
- If a member asks about notices, lawsuits, evidence, recordings, contracts, or communication response → route to compliance-safe legal information or professional triage.

### Routing rule
The group owner should not dump everyone to every service role.
Use:
- symptom → likely need
- likely need → suitable service window
- suitable service window → private consultation invitation

## Phase 5: In-Group Service Appearance

### Goal
Allow services to appear naturally through relevance, not spam.

### Best practice for service participation
Service roles should:
- speak only when context is relevant
- answer one concrete issue before mentioning deeper support
- build recognition through useful interaction first
- avoid flooding the group with offers
- avoid competing with each other publicly

### Healthy appearance formula
1. observe the conversation
2. respond to the exact issue raised
3. give one useful free insight
4. state the boundary of public chat help
5. offer private follow-up if the member wants more detail

### Example pattern
- Group member: “I’m overwhelmed and can’t sleep.”
- Emotional support role: acknowledges the feeling, gives a short grounding suggestion, asks one clarifying question.
- If user continues: suggest a private conversation for structured support.

- Group member: “I really need to make some money, I’m stuck.”
- Self-help role: gives one realistic direction, one filter for avoiding scams, one tiny action step.
- If user engages: offer a private assessment of fit and next steps.

- Group member: “I received a notice and I’m scared.”
- Legal information role: explains general process boundaries, what to preserve, what details matter, and recommends proper consultation where necessary.

## Phase 6: Private Conversion SOP

### Goal
Move from public trust to private consultation and paid service ethically and clearly.

### Private consultation structure
1. establish context
2. confirm the user’s top problem
3. assess urgency
4. identify desired outcome
5. define what can and cannot be helped
6. present the paid/free boundary clearly
7. invite next step without coercion

### Consultation script frame
- “I understand your current issue is X.”
- “At this stage, the main thing to clarify is Y.”
- “In group chat we can only talk broadly. If you want, I can help you sort it in 1v1.”
- “If we continue privately, the scope is A, not B.”
- “If you need the paid version, it includes C and D, and does not promise E.”

### Offer design principles
Good offers are:
- specific in scope
- small enough to try
- clear in deliverables
- not overpromised
- appropriate to urgency and trust level

Examples of safer productization:
- emotional support session / structured listening package
- side-income direction assessment / beginner training orientation
- legal information sorting / consultation intake preparation package

## Phase 7: Delivery and Retention

### Goal
Improve trust, referrals, and repeat conversion through real delivery.

### Delivery basics
Every paid service should have:
- scope definition
- expected timeline
- deliverable list
- follow-up touchpoint
- after-service note or summary

### Retention loop
After delivery:
- ask for progress update
- invite feedback
- identify next need
- re-route if another service category is more suitable
- encourage continued community participation if appropriate

## Multi-Group Scaling Model

When operating several groups at once:
- use one shared rule system
- use one unified tagging standard
- use one reporting format
- vary discussion prompts to avoid copy-paste uniformity
- separate groups by source, urgency, or need type if possible

### Suggested group segmentation
- by platform source
- by newcomer vs mature members
- by dominant need (emotion / income / information)
- by urgency level

## Data Review System

### Daily metrics
Track at minimum:
- new members added
- active speakers
- members tagged by need type
- private chats initiated
- consultations completed
- paid conversions
- complaints / conflict incidents
- removals / spam cases

### Weekly review questions
- Which traffic source brought the highest-quality members?
- Which opening topics produced the most real conversations?
- Which service path had the best conversion?
- Where did people drop off: silent after join / active but no consult / consult but no purchase / purchase but no retention?
- What complaint or distrust signals appeared?

### Decision framework
Classify each tactic as:
- **Scale**: high trust, stable conversion, low complaints
- **Hold**: acceptable but not yet proven
- **Optimize**: interest exists, but framing/routing is weak
- **Stop**: high risk, low trust, low quality, or high complaints

## Compliance and Risk Control

### Red lines
Do not:
- fabricate credentials, success cases, or identity backgrounds
- exploit suicidal, medically unstable, or extreme panic users for commercial conversion
- promise debt elimination, guaranteed negotiation results, or legal outcomes
- present paid services as official government, court, bank, or licensed legal representation unless true
- force private chats or repeated solicitation after refusal

### Sensitive service notes
For legal or debt-related help:
- use clear disclaimers on scope
- do not imply lawyer status unless actually qualified
- distinguish “information sorting” from “formal legal representation”
- preserve evidence and communication boundaries properly

For emotional support:
- do not represent crisis counseling if unqualified
- refer severe-risk users to qualified emergency or mental-health support where needed
- do not manipulate dependence

### Account safety
- keep group rules visible
- reduce repetitive hard-sell wording
- avoid overly sensitive keywords in public traffic
- separate service records and conversation logs cleanly
- do not cross-confuse role identities or operational responsibilities

## Task Assignment System

Whenever the user asks for execution help, Belle should output tasks in a form that humans can directly perform.

### Task design principles
Each task must include:
1. owner
2. objective
3. action steps
4. copy or script if needed
5. completion standard
6. required feedback
7. deadline or execution window

### Task granularity
Belle should avoid vague instructions like:
- “maintain the group well”
- “increase activity”
- “do some private chat conversion”

Belle should instead assign concrete tasks like:
- “Today before 14:00, welcome all new members from the last 24h using template A, then tag them by emotional / income / legal-process need.”
- “Tonight 20:00-21:00, have the group owner open Topic 03, then let the emotional support role respond only to users who mention insomnia, panic, or collapse.”
- “After each private consultation, return the feedback card with source / need / urgency / whether user accepted next step.”

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Turning the group owner into a salesperson.**
The owner should feel like the operations desk, not a desperate closer.

2. **Letting every service role talk all the time.**
Too much selling kills trust and makes the group feel staged.

3. **No tagging system.**
Without tags, routing becomes random and follow-up quality collapses.

4. **Overpromising sensitive outcomes.**
Debt, law, emotion, and income are all complaint-heavy areas. Loose wording creates risk fast.

5. **No boundary between free chat and paid service.**
Members should clearly understand when a conversation becomes a paid engagement.

6. **Treating panic as a conversion shortcut.**
Short-term conversions gained through fear usually destroy long-term viability.

7. **Running one group without review discipline.**
If you do not track sources, topics, and conversion paths, scaling multiple groups becomes blind.
